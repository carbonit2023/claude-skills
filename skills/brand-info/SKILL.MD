Carbon Brand Information
Internal brand reference document

1. Core Identity
   Brand Name: Carbon
   Tagline: Jewel of Emotion
   Founding Story: Carbon was created to redefine fine jewellery for a new generation of luxury consumers. With deep roots in the natural diamond industry, the founders recognised a shift towards design, innovation, and conscious luxury. Carbon was built as a design-led maison that brings together craftsmanship, scale, and modernity through lab-grown diamonds without compromising on emotion, aspiration, or luxury.
   Mission: To redefine fine jewellery for the modern luxury consumer through exceptional design, craftsmanship, trust, and emotionally resonant pieces that feel contemporary, elevated, and aspirational, while building heirloom pieces that can be passed down through generations.
   Vision: To become India's leading design-led luxury jewellery maison and a category-defining benchmark that other jewellers aspire to follow.
   Unique Selling Proposition (USP): A design-first luxury jewellery brand offering lab-grown diamonds with a strong focus on bridal, statement, and high-expression pieces, backed by craftsmanship, certification, and a premium in-store experience.
   Why customers choose Carbon over competitors: Strong design language and modern luxury aesthetic; heavy bridal and statement jewellery capability; lab-grown Polki leadership; large ready inventory; certified diamonds and hallmarking; strong value for size and quality; premium customer experience.
2. Product & Pricing
   Primary Products: Lab-grown diamond jewellery, bridal jewellery, Polki jewellery, solitaires, colour diamond jewellery, coloured stone jewellery, men's jewellery, and everyday fine jewellery.
   Categories: Bridal and Occasion; Signature Collections; Everyday / Essentials; Men's Jewellery; Lifestyle Add-ons; Solitaires; Lab-grown Polki; Colour Diamond Jewellery; and Colour Stone Jewellery.
   Style: Modern luxury, bold yet refined, couture-inspired, and emotionally expressive. A strong differentiator is that Carbon uses fancy-shaped diamonds more than round diamonds, making the designs more distinctive and fashion-forward.
   Price Range: Rs 15,000 to Rs 50 lakh and above.
   Best-Selling Products: Rings are the strongest sellers, especially solitaires. Earrings come next. Necklaces sell in lower volume but contribute a healthy margin to revenue.
   Slow-Moving Inventory: Highly niche or over-designed pieces without clear positioning or heavy bridal sets.
   Certifications / Trust Factors: IGI-certified diamonds, BIS hallmarking, 100 percent lifetime exchange, 90 percent buyback, strong industry legacy of 4 generations, and a premium service experience.
3. Locations & Store Insights
   Branches (City + Area): Ahmedabad, Gurugram, Noida, Mumbai, and Jamnagar.
   Per Branch Customer Type: Ahmedabad: bridal buyers, family purchases, High-Net-Worth Individuals (HNWIs), first-time diamond buyers, and occasion shoppers. Gurugram: HNWIs, corporate professionals, luxury buyers, residents of ultra-premium societies such as Camellias, and design-conscious couples. Noida: aspirational premium buyers and wedding shoppers. Mumbai: fashion-forward, design-led luxury consumers. Jamnagar: family-led purchases and wedding and occasion buyers.
   Footfall Type: A mix of planned visits, bridal appointments, referrals, walk-ins, residential society exposure, and digital-driven store visits.
   Nearby Competitors: Varies by city and micro-market, including legacy premium jewellers, bridal jewellers, and emerging lab-grown brands.
4. Target Audience
   Age Group: 25 to 50 years.
   Gender Focus: Primarily women, with a growing relevance for men's jewellery and gifting.
   Income Level: Upper middle class, affluent households, and HNWIs.
   Occasions: Weddings, engagements, festive purchases, gifting, milestone celebrations, and statement event dressing.
   Customer Mindset: The customer wants the best of everything and values craftsmanship, design, service, and the overall luxury experience. At the same time, many still hesitate to pay a high price for lab-grown jewellery, even when they understand the category, so reassurance and experience remain critical for conversion.
   Preferred Language & Tone: Premium, polished, aspirational, emotionally intelligent, and adapted to city context using English, Hindi, Gujarati, or a blend where suitable.
5. Offers & Sales Strategy
   Current Offers: Selective and controlled. Carbon does not rely on loud public discounting.
   Past Offers (Worked): Flat value-led offers tend to perform better than conditional voucher mechanics. Strong product storytelling and in-store negotiation also help conversion.
   Past Offers (Failed): The 'win a solitaire' billboard campaign did not generate meaningful scans or traction. Conditional vouchers such as Rs 25,000 off on a purchase of Rs 1,25,000 have also underperformed.
   Festival Offers Strategy: Premium, occasion-led, and storytelling-based rather than discount-heavy.
   Discount Limits: Flexible depending on the customer and situation. In-store teams may go deeper to rope in a customer when required, but discounts are never advertised publicly. Any discounting is typically adjusted more on the diamond value than on gold or making.
   Inventory Push Strategy: Use styling, curation, storytelling, and store-led selling to move inventory rather than clearance-led communication.
6. Customer Data & Behaviour
   Customer Segments: Bridal buyers, everyday buyers, gifting customers, HNWIs, first-time buyers, and milestone purchase customers.
   Average Ticket Size: Approximately Rs 80,000 to Rs 1,00,000.
   Purchase Frequency: Occasional and lifecycle-based, with both planned and emotional purchase moments.
   Peak Buying Seasons: Wedding seasons, national festivals, and key regional festivals relevant to each store city.
   Buying Patterns: Bridal and milestone purchases remain key, but Ahmedabad also sees casual buying at times.
   Top 3 Selling Categories: Solitaires are the strongest category overall, particularly in rings and earrings. Rings and earrings are the top recurring product types.
7. Past Marketing Performance
   Overall Reality: There was no proper structured strategy earlier, which led to a waste of time and money.
   WhatsApp Campaigns: No reliable benchmark data currently available.
   Instagram: What works best still needs to be properly established, but using a face helps and real people are likely to perform better than generic visuals.
   Influencer Learnings: Carbon has not meaningfully explored influencer collaborations yet, so this needs to be identified and tested strategically.
   Ads: There is no fixed monthly ad budget at present, and past performance is not structured enough to serve as a benchmark.
8. Content Assets
   Product Photos: Need to be generated and organised in a stronger, systematic manner.
   Videos: Need to be generated and planned with better consistency.
   Store Photos: Available and can be improved further as a content bank.
   Customer Testimonials: Can be shot and built into a stronger trust asset over time.
   Influencer Content: Needs to be identified, produced, and tested.
9. Brand Voice & Communication Rules
   Tone: Premium, refined, aspirational, polished, and warm.
   Words to Use: Use relevant market-facing words that align with what is currently selling and resonating in the market, while staying true to Carbon's luxury positioning. Examples include craftsmanship, brilliance, statement, iconic, modern, heirloom, celebration, refined, and design-led.
   Words to Avoid: Cheap, artificial, imitation, discount-heavy, budget, and overly mass-market language.
   CTA Style: Soft luxury calls to action such as Explore, Visit, Discover, Enquire, and Book an Appointment.
   Emoji Usage: Minimal and selective.
10. Business Constraints
    Monthly Marketing Budget: No fixed monthly budget currently.
    Staff Capacity: Store capacity varies by team size and season.
    Walk-in Handling Limit: Depends on store strength, staffing, and appointment load.
    Offer Approval Process: All approvals come from Head Office and the founders.
    Operational Limitations: Low customer awareness, low walk-ins, and the need for stronger demand generation and education at the store level.
11. Competitor Analysis
    Competitor 1
    Name: Local premium legacy jewellers
    Strength: Trust, legacy, large customer base, wedding credibility
    Weakness: Often less contemporary in design language and branding
    Offers they run: Festive schemes, exchange offers, occasion promotions
    Platforms they dominate: Offline trust, local market recall, traditional jewellery purchase journeys

Competitor 2
Name: Emerging lab-grown jewellery brands
Strength: Category relevance, price-led appeal, modern storytelling
Weakness: Often lack true luxury positioning, depth in bridal, or strong store experience
Offers: Introductory price-led communication, online-first promotions, awareness campaigns
12. Important Dates & Calendar
Key Festivals: All national festivals, plus all regional festivals relevant to the markets in which Carbon stores operate.
Wedding Seasons: Major Indian wedding cycles across the year, especially peak bridal periods.
Local Events: Society activations, exhibitions, premium community events, trunk shows, and city-level luxury gatherings.
Store Events: Private previews, bridal events, trunk shows, and curated in-store experiences.
13. Partnerships & Collaborations
Past Collaborations: Brand and event-led collaborations have been explored selectively. Have collaborated with Ficci Flo ( Ladies Wing ), Jito Ladies Wing, Corporate connections, BNI, Gaurav Gupta ( Apparel Designer )
Potential Partners: Designers, stylists, wedding planners, premium residential societies, hospitality partners, editors, and carefully chosen creators.
Target Collaboration Type: Luxury-aligned, brand-building, trust-enhancing, and experience-led collaborations.
14. Business Goals
Monthly Revenue Target: Each store should aim for at least Rs 1 crore in monthly sales. Ahmedabad should aim for approximately Rs 1.5 crore to Rs 2 crore per month.
Growth Goal: To become the benchmark brand before all jewellers and establish Carbon as the category leader in modern luxury and lab-grown jewellery.
Primary Focus Areas: Brand awareness first, followed by walk-ins, repeat customers, and then inventory movement.
